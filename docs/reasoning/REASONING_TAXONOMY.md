# Complete Taxonomy of Reasoning Styles

## Overview

This document catalogs all major types of reasoning, organized by category. Each type represents a distinct cognitive approach to problem-solving.

---

## 1. Logical Reasoning

### 1.1 Deductive Reasoning
**Definition**: Reasoning from general principles to specific conclusions
**Certainty**: Conclusions are certain if premises are true
**Examples**:
- All humans are mortal; Socrates is human → Socrates is mortal
- If P then Q; P is true → Q is true

**Formal Systems**:
- Propositional logic
- First-order logic
- Modal logic

**Applications**: Theorem proving, verification, formal proofs

---

### 1.2 Inductive Reasoning
**Definition**: Reasoning from specific observations to general principles
**Certainty**: Conclusions are probable, not certain
**Examples**:
- The sun has risen every day → The sun will rise tomorrow
- All observed swans are white → All swans are white (falsified!)

**Types**:
- Enumerative induction (repeated observations)
- Eliminative induction (rule out alternatives)
- Statistical induction (from sample to population)

**Applications**: Scientific hypothesis generation, pattern discovery

---

### 1.3 Abductive Reasoning
**Definition**: Inference to the best explanation
**Certainty**: Plausible, but not certain
**Examples**:
- Grass is wet → It probably rained (best explanation)
- Patient has fever, cough → Probably has flu (diagnosis)

**Process**:
1. Observe surprising fact
2. Generate possible explanations
3. Select most plausible

**Applications**: Diagnosis, debugging, detective work, scientific discovery

---

### 1.4 Analogical Reasoning
**Definition**: Reasoning by comparing similar structures
**Form**: A is to B as C is to D
**Examples**:
- Atom is like solar system (structure mapping)
- Learning to ride motorcycle after learning bicycle

**Components**:
- Source domain (known)
- Target domain (unknown)
- Structural mapping
- Inference transfer

**Applications**: Learning transfer, problem solving, explanation

---

## 2. Mathematical Reasoning

### 2.1 Algebraic Reasoning
**Focus**: Symbolic manipulation, equations, variables
**Operations**:
- Equation solving
- Factorization
- Simplification
- Substitution

**Examples**: Solve x² + 5x + 6 = 0, factor polynomials

---

### 2.2 Geometric Reasoning
**Focus**: Spatial properties, shapes, transformations
**Concepts**:
- Congruence and similarity
- Angles and distances
- Area and volume
- Transformations (rotation, reflection, translation)

**Examples**: Prove triangles congruent, calculate circle area

---

### 2.3 Arithmetic Reasoning
**Focus**: Numerical operations and relationships
**Operations**: Addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, exponents
**Examples**: Mental math, estimation, number sense

---

### 2.4 Topological Reasoning
**Focus**: Properties preserved under continuous transformation
**Concepts**:
- Connectedness
- Compactness
- Continuity
- Homeomorphism

**Examples**: Euler characteristic, knot theory, network topology

---

### 2.5 Statistical Reasoning
**Focus**: Data analysis, probability, inference
**Concepts**:
- Distributions
- Correlation vs causation
- Sampling
- Hypothesis testing

**Examples**: Is this difference significant? What's the expected value?

---

### 2.6 Combinatorial Reasoning
**Focus**: Counting, arrangements, selections
**Concepts**:
- Permutations and combinations
- Graph theory
- Optimization

**Examples**: How many ways to arrange 5 people? Traveling salesman

---

## 3. Spatial/Visual Reasoning

### 3.1 Spatial Relations Reasoning
**Focus**: Relationships between objects in space
**Relations**:
- Above/below, left/right
- Inside/outside
- Near/far
- Between

**Examples**: Map reading, spatial navigation

---

### 3.2 Mental Rotation
**Focus**: Imagining objects rotated in space
**Tasks**:
- 2D rotation
- 3D rotation
- Mirror images

**Examples**: Block rotation tests, mechanical reasoning

---

### 3.3 Visual Pattern Recognition
**Focus**: Identifying visual regularities
**Patterns**:
- Symmetry (reflection, rotation, translation)
- Repetition
- Scaling
- Color/shape patterns

**Examples**: IQ matrix puzzles, tessellations

---

### 3.4 Transformation Reasoning
**Focus**: Understanding how operations change objects
**Transformations**:
- Geometric (rotate, scale, translate)
- Topological (stretch, bend)
- Color/texture changes
- Compositional (combine, separate)

**Examples**: ARC-AGI tasks, image manipulation

---

## 4. Temporal Reasoning

### 4.1 Sequential Reasoning
**Focus**: Order and succession
**Concepts**:
- Before/after
- First/last
- Next/previous

**Examples**: Event ordering, process steps

---

### 4.2 Causal Reasoning
**Focus**: Cause and effect relationships
**Types**:
- Direct causation (A causes B)
- Indirect causation (A causes B causes C)
- Common cause (C causes both A and B)
- Preventive causation (A prevents B)

**Examples**: Why did this happen? What will happen if...?

---

### 4.3 Temporal Logic
**Focus**: Reasoning about time-dependent facts
**Operators**:
- Always, eventually, until, next
- Since, before, after

**Examples**: System verification, planning

---

### 4.4 Counterfactual Reasoning
**Focus**: What would have happened if...
**Form**: If X had been different, Y would have been different
**Examples**: "If I had studied, I would have passed"

---

## 5. Probabilistic/Uncertain Reasoning

### 5.1 Bayesian Reasoning
**Focus**: Updating beliefs with evidence
**Formula**: P(H|E) = P(E|H) * P(H) / P(E)
**Examples**: Medical diagnosis, spam filtering

---

### 5.2 Fuzzy Reasoning
**Focus**: Reasoning with degrees of truth
**Values**: Not just true/false, but 0.0 to 1.0
**Examples**: "Tall person" (how tall?), temperature control

---

### 5.3 Evidential Reasoning
**Focus**: Combining multiple sources of evidence
**Approaches**:
- Dempster-Shafer theory
- Certainty factors
- Belief networks

**Examples**: Intelligence analysis, sensor fusion

---

## 6. Search and Optimization

### 6.1 Systematic Search
**Types**:
- Breadth-first search
- Depth-first search
- Best-first search
- A* search

**Applications**: Path finding, game playing, planning

---

### 6.2 Constraint Satisfaction
**Focus**: Finding values that satisfy all constraints
**Techniques**:
- Backtracking
- Forward checking
- Arc consistency

**Examples**: Sudoku, scheduling, resource allocation

---

### 6.3 Optimization Reasoning
**Focus**: Finding best solution
**Types**:
- Linear programming
- Convex optimization
- Evolutionary algorithms
- Gradient descent

**Examples**: Minimize cost, maximize efficiency

---

### 6.4 Heuristic Reasoning
**Focus**: Rules of thumb for efficient solving
**Examples**:
- Greedy algorithms
- Hill climbing
- Simulated annealing

---

## 7. Compositional Reasoning

### 7.1 Hierarchical Reasoning
**Focus**: Breaking down into levels
**Structure**:
- Top-down decomposition
- Bottom-up composition
- Abstraction levels

**Examples**: Software architecture, organizational structure

---

### 7.2 Modular Reasoning
**Focus**: Independent components
**Principles**:
- Separation of concerns
- Interface contracts
- Composability

**Examples**: Software modules, mechanical systems

---

### 7.3 Part-Whole Reasoning
**Focus**: Relationships between parts and wholes
**Properties**:
- Mereology (formal study)
- Emergent properties
- Functional roles

**Examples**: Body has heart, car has engine

---

## 8. Categorical/Taxonomic Reasoning

### 8.1 Classification
**Focus**: Grouping by shared properties
**Operations**:
- Is-a relationships
- Category membership
- Prototypes vs exemplars

**Examples**: Dog is a mammal, tomato is a fruit

---

### 8.2 Abstraction
**Focus**: Extracting common features
**Levels**:
- Specific → General
- Concrete → Abstract
- Instance → Type

**Examples**: "Vehicle" abstracts cars, bikes, planes

---

### 8.3 Subsumption Reasoning
**Focus**: Inheritance hierarchies
**Relationships**:
- Superclass/subclass
- Property inheritance
- Specialization

**Examples**: Taxonomies, ontologies

---

## 9. Meta-Cognitive Reasoning

### 9.1 Reasoning About Reasoning
**Focus**: Thinking about thinking
**Aspects**:
- Strategy selection
- Performance monitoring
- Error detection

**Examples**: "Am I solving this correctly?" "What method should I use?"

---

### 9.2 Planning
**Focus**: Determining sequence of actions
**Types**:
- Forward planning (from start to goal)
- Backward planning (from goal to start)
- Hierarchical planning

**Examples**: Trip planning, project management

---

### 9.3 Self-Monitoring
**Focus**: Tracking own reasoning process
**Aspects**:
- Confidence assessment
- Progress evaluation
- Resource allocation

---

### 9.4 Strategy Synthesis
**Focus**: Creating new problem-solving approaches
**Process**:
- Analyze problem requirements
- Identify available methods
- Combine into strategy

**Examples**: Supe's meta-solver creating new strategies

---

## 10. Social/Pragmatic Reasoning

### 10.1 Theory of Mind
**Focus**: Understanding others' mental states
**Aspects**:
- Beliefs (what they think)
- Desires (what they want)
- Intentions (what they plan)

**Examples**: "He believes the door is locked" "She wants ice cream"

---

### 10.2 Social Reasoning
**Focus**: Understanding social dynamics
**Aspects**:
- Social norms
- Power structures
- Group dynamics

**Examples**: Politics, negotiations, etiquette

---

### 10.3 Pragmatic Reasoning
**Focus**: Practical, context-dependent inference
**Examples**:
- Conversational implicature
- Reading between the lines
- Understanding intent

---

## 11. Creative/Generative Reasoning

### 11.1 Lateral Thinking
**Focus**: Indirect, creative approaches
**Techniques**:
- Challenge assumptions
- Random entry
- Reversal

**Examples**: Edward de Bono's puzzles

---

### 11.2 Conceptual Blending
**Focus**: Merging concepts to create new ones
**Process**:
- Select inputs
- Find correspondences
- Project to blend
- Elaborate

**Examples**: "Computer virus", "time is money"

---

### 11.3 Divergent Thinking
**Focus**: Generating many solutions
**Contrast**: Convergent thinking (one best answer)
**Examples**: Brainstorming, creative problem solving

---

## 12. Domain-Specific Reasoning

### 12.1 Physical Reasoning
**Focus**: Understanding physical world
**Concepts**:
- Object permanence
- Gravity, friction
- Support relations
- Containment

**Examples**: Naive physics, intuitive physics

---

### 12.2 Biological Reasoning
**Focus**: Living systems
**Concepts**:
- Growth and reproduction
- Inheritance
- Ecosystems
- Evolution

---

### 12.3 Psychological Reasoning
**Focus**: Human behavior and mind
**Concepts**:
- Motivation
- Emotion
- Learning
- Personality

---

### 12.4 Economic Reasoning
**Focus**: Resources, trade, value
**Concepts**:
- Supply and demand
- Incentives
- Game theory
- Utility

---

## 13. Program Synthesis/Computational Reasoning

### 13.1 Program Synthesis
**Focus**: Generating programs from specifications
**Approaches**:
- Inductive synthesis (from examples)
- Deductive synthesis (from logical spec)
- Program sketching

**Examples**: Generate function from input-output pairs

---

### 13.2 Algorithmic Reasoning
**Focus**: Understanding and creating algorithms
**Aspects**:
- Correctness
- Complexity
- Optimization

---

### 13.3 Type-Level Reasoning
**Focus**: Reasoning about types and categories
**Examples**: Type inference, category theory

---

## 14. Specialized Reasoning Types

### 14.1 Diagrammatic Reasoning
**Focus**: Using diagrams to reason
**Types**:
- Venn diagrams
- Flow charts
- Graph diagrams
- Euler diagrams

---

### 14.2 Case-Based Reasoning
**Focus**: Solving new problems by adapting old solutions
**Process**:
1. Retrieve similar case
2. Reuse solution
3. Revise for new context
4. Retain in case library

---

### 14.3 Model-Based Reasoning
**Focus**: Using mental models to simulate
**Examples**:
- Mental simulation
- Thought experiments
- "What if" scenarios

---

### 14.4 Non-Monotonic Reasoning
**Focus**: Reasoning that can be revised
**Property**: Adding information can invalidate previous conclusions
**Examples**: "Birds fly" + "Penguins are birds" → "Penguins don't fly"

---
